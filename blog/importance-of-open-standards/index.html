The Importance of
Open Standards

Why the film industry must embrace open file formats to protect the legacy and interoperability of creative work.

The film industry is notoriously reliant on proprietary software. From editing timelines to screenplay drafts, vast amounts of critical data are stored in closed, undocumented formats. This creates a fragile ecosystem where long-term archival access is never guaranteed.

Open standards offer a robust alternative. When a file format is open—meaning its specifications are publicly available and unrestricted—anyone can develop software to read and write it. This guarantees that data remains accessible regardless of which software company dominates the market.

The Fountain Revolution

In the screenwriting world, the Fountain markup language is the beacon of open standards. Because it is simply plain text structured with specific rules, a Fountain file will be readable as long as computers can process basic characters.

Adopting open standards ensures true interoperability. A writer can draft in one open-source editor, pass the file to a producer using a different platform, and never worry about translation errors or missing fonts.

"Open standards are the bedrock of digital preservation."

By advocating for and utilizing tools built around open standards, creators protect not just their own work, but the collective memory of the entire industry.
