Embracing the
Fountain Syntax

A comprehensive look at why the Fountain markup language is the most liberating development in screenwriting technology.

Formatting a screenplay has traditionally involved constantly tabbing between elements: Scene Heading, Action, Character, Dialogue. It's a mechanical process that can interrupt the organic flow of writing.

Enter Fountain, a plain-text markup language designed specifically for screenwriting. Fountain allows you to write a perfectly formatted screenplay using nothing but simple typographic conventions. A line in all caps is recognized as a Character. A line starting with INT. or EXT. is a Scene Heading. It's incredibly intuitive.

The Freedom to Write Anywhere

Because Fountain is just plain text, you can write in any application you choose. You can jot down a scene in the Notes app on your phone, draft an act in a basic text editor on a Chromebook, and then bring it all together on your main workstation.

The magic happens when you use a Fountain-aware application. These specialized tools parse the plain text and instantly render it into standard industry format, complete with correct margins and pagination, ready for export to PDF.

"Fountain removes the friction between the brain and the page."

Adopting Fountain isn't just about learning a new syntax; it's about untethering yourself from bulky, expensive software suites and rediscovering the joy of pure, unadulterated typing.
