Offline Writing,
Still Matters.

Cloud editors are handy. But for a screenplay you might live with for a year, keeping the file on your own PC still wins.

TL;DR

Writing offline is faster, more private and more durable. ActOne Screenplay and FountTUI keep your Fountain files as local text you own. No required account, no upload to write or export, no risk if a service changes its price. You can still share a PDF or FDX when you need to.

I like working on a train. No signal for an hour, just the script. On a cloud editor that hour is stressful. On a local editor it is the best hour of the day. That contrast is why I still care about offline, even in 2026.

Cloud writing tools have improved a lot. Real time collaboration is nice. Auto sync is convenient. But a screenplay is not a short doc you finish in one sitting. It is a long project you open hundreds of times, often in places with bad wifi, and you want it to open instantly every time.

What offline really gives you

  • Speed. The app opens when you click it, not when the server responds. In ActOne, recent projects appear immediately because they are on your drive.
  • Privacy. Your draft stays on your PC. ActOne only uses the network to check for updates and to send a crash report if you allow it. It never uploads your story to write.
  • Ownership. A Fountain file is plain text. You can copy it, back it up with any tool, and open it ten years from now. You are not tied to a file format that only one site understands.
  • Stability. A local app does not change its price or limit while you are in the second act. Your workflow does not depend on a browser update.

What you give up — and how to get it back

The usual reason people pick cloud is collaboration. That is fair. You can still collaborate without living in the cloud the whole time.

With Fountain, the script itself is the collaboration format. Keep the source as .fountain or .actone locally, then share a PDF or FDX when you need notes. Your collaborator opens the export, you keep the source. Snapshots in ActOne give you a local version history so you can compare drafts without needing a cloud revision slider.

If you truly need live co writing for a day, you can edit a shared copy in a browser that day and pull the final Fountain back to ActOne. Most weeks, though, you will be writing alone. Offline is the right default for alone time.

A simple local workflow that works

  1. Keep one folder for the project. The Fountain file or .actone bundle lives there, along with notes.
  2. Back it up like any other folder. External drive, file history, or a private sync folder you control.
  3. Use snapshots in ActOne before big rewrites. Tag them like “before act two reorder.”
  4. When you need notes, export PDF or FDX and share that, not the source.

This is boring in a good way. No dashboards, no permissions to manage. Just files you can see.

Offline does not mean isolated

Fountain helps here. Because it is plain text, you can open the same file in ActOne on Windows and in FountTUI in the terminal, or hand it to a friend on a Mac who uses Beat. No conversion step. When you want AI help, ActOne uses local models via Ollama or your own API key, again without sending the whole script to a shared workspace.

Keep the source local. Share the export. You get both privacy and collaboration without having to choose one forever.

How ActOne and FountTUI handle this

ActOne Screenplay is a desktop workspace for Windows and Linux. It edits Fountain directly, keeps snapshots locally, and renders PDFs with a Rust engine. FountTUI is the terminal companion. Same Fountain files, even smaller footprint. Neither needs an account to start writing.

We built them this way because we write with them. The fastest way to lose a thought is to wait for a page to load. The safest way to lose a script is to keep it only where someone else can delete it. Local first solves both.

FAQ

Can I write screenplays offline?

Yes. ActOne works fully offline on Windows and Linux. So does FountTUI. You can write, save, and export without internet.

Do I lose collaboration if I work offline?

No. Keep your Fountain source local and share PDFs or FDX exports when you need feedback. You stay in control of the master file.

Is Fountain safe for long term storage?

Yes. It is plain text. It will be readable longer than any proprietary binary. An .actone bundle is a ZIP with Fountain inside, so you are never locked in.
